# Mission: ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English)

## Why
Nitan wants his everyday writing — personal blog posts, product copy, and social posts
for an engineering audience — to be unambiguous on first read. STE is the most rigorously
tested controlled-English standard in existence, so it is being used here as a **discipline
and a diagnostic**, not as a compliance target. The goal is writing that cannot be
misread, while keeping his own voice.

## Success looks like
- He can spot, name, and fix the common STE violations in a draft of his own prose (long sentences, multi-idea sentences, noun clusters, vague verbs, nominalised actions).
- He can rewrite a paragraph of his own product copy into compliant STE, then decide consciously which rules to relax for voice.
- He knows what STE actually specifies — 53 rules in 9 sections plus a ~900-word dictionary — and does not confuse it with generic "write simply" advice.
- He can write a genuinely compliant procedure (install steps, a runbook, a README quickstart) when the text is instructional.

## Constraints
- Starting from zero: he knows the name and nothing else.
- Short sessions. One tangible win each, completable in a few minutes.
- Wants all four modes: rule-by-rule teaching, rewrite drills with feedback, work on his real text, and printable reference sheets.
- He has a strong, established writing voice (see `NOTES.md`). Teaching must never quietly flatten it.

## Out of scope
- Aerospace/defence documentation practice: S1000D, ATA chapters, IPC/AMM structure.
- STE certification, checker-vendor tooling procurement, corporate rollout process.
- Building an STE linter (interesting, but not this mission).
