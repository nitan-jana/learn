Reference · ASD-STE100

Glossary

The words the standard uses about itself. Every lesson in this workspace uses these terms in exactly this sense.

Definitions are drawn from the official sources listed in RESOURCES.md, chiefly About STE and the official FAQ. Where a definition is this workspace's own shorthand rather than the standard's wording, it is marked (ours).

The standard

TermMeaning
ASD-STE100 The full name of the standard: Simplified Technical English, Standard for Technical Documentation, published by ASD (AeroSpace, Security and Defence Industries Association of Europe). Free to download as a PDF.
STE The usual abbreviation. Note it is Simplified Technical English, not "simple English" — the subject matter stays technical; only the language is controlled.
Controlled natural language A subset of a natural language with a restricted vocabulary and a restricted grammar, defined to remove ambiguity. STE is the best known example in industry.
Issue 9 The current edition, released 15 January 2025. It added no new rules but reworded 31 of the 53, updated 555 dictionary entries, and aligned terminology with ISO 1087-1:2019 (which is where "technical nouns" and "technical verbs" come from).
AECMA Simplified English The original name. Developed in the late 1970s for aircraft maintenance documentation and first released in 1986; renamed when AECMA became ASD.
Part 1 / Writing Rules 53 rules in 9 sections, covering word choice, grammar, sentence structure, and style.
Part 2 / Dictionary About 900 approved words, plus roughly 1,200 unapproved words each with a suggested replacement.

Words

TermMeaning
Approved word A word in the dictionary. Each one is approved with one meaning and one part of speech. "Test" is approved as a noun, so you write do a test, not test the unit, unless the dictionary also approves the verb.
Unapproved word A word the dictionary lists specifically to forbid, with an approved alternative beside it. This is the part most writers find surprising: the list tells you what to write instead.
Technical Name (Issue 9: technical noun) A noun outside the dictionary that you are permitted to use because it names a real part, tool, material, or entity in your subject matter — for example fuel pump, or in your domain, connection pooler. It must come from official documentation, drawings, a company glossary, or a terminology database.
Technical Verb The same permission for verbs that name a real manufacturing or technical process, where no approved verb will do. The FAQ tells you to limit both as much as possible.
Agent "The person or thing that does the action" (rule 3.6). The test for the passive voice is whether the sentence answers "by whom, or by what?"
Active voice The grammatical subject is also the agent: A switching relay connects the circuits. Required everywhere in STE, with one exception (below).
Passive voice The subject receives the action: The circuits are connected by a switching relay. Permitted only in descriptive text, and only when the agent is genuinely unknown. See Passive to active.
Imperative form The command form of a verb: Remove the cover. Rule 5.3 requires it for instructions, and it is method 3 for killing a passive in a procedure.
Noun cluster A string of nouns used as one compound noun. STE allows a maximum of three words. Main gear door retraction winch handle is the standard's own example of what goes wrong.
Nominalisation (ours) A verb turned into a noun: perform an installation of instead of install. STE pushes you back to the verb.
Phrasal verb A verb plus a particle whose meaning is not the sum of its parts (put up with, turn down). Banned, because non-native readers cannot decode them and dictionaries disagree.

Kinds of text

TermMeaning
Procedure Text that tells the reader to do something: steps, instructions, a runbook, a quickstart. The strictest rules apply here — including the 20-word sentence limit and mandatory active voice.
Descriptive text Text that explains how something works or what it is. The limits relax slightly: 25 words per sentence, and the passive voice is allowed when the agent is genuinely unknown.
Warning / Caution Safety text. A warning covers injury to people; a caution covers damage to equipment. STE specifies their wording and their position — before the step, never after.
Checker Software that flags rule and dictionary violations. Useful, but no checker decides whether a sentence is clear; that judgement stays with the writer.
