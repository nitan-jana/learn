Reference · ASD-STE100 rule 3.6

Passive to active

Four methods, one test. Keep this beside you until the conversion is automatic.

The test

Ask of the verb: “by whom, or by what?” If the sentence answers, it is passive. The word by is the giveaway, but a passive can hide the agent completely (“The dimensions are given in the table”) — and those are the ones that matter, because the missing agent is often the information the reader needed.

Not passive: a past participle used as an adjective. Examine all parts of the disassembled unit and When the unit is fully disassembled, clean all the parts are both fine. Rule 3.3 permits the past participle before a noun, or after to be, to become, to stay, to show the condition of something. Software checkers routinely get this wrong; so will the drill in lesson 2.

The four methods

MethodWhenExample
1
Promote the agent		 The sentence names the agent, usually after by. The circuits are connected by a switching relay.
→ A switching relay connects the circuits.
2
Use the real verb		 The passive construction carries no information, and a better verb is sitting in the sentence already. These values are used by the computer to calculate the energy consumption.
→ The computer calculates the energy consumption from these values.
3
Command the reader		 Procedural writing. The agent is the reader, so address them. Oil and grease are to be removed with a degreasing agent.
→ Remove oil and grease with a degreasing agent.
4
Say you or we		 The agent is missing. Use you if it is the reader, we if it is your organisation. On the ground, the valve can be opened with the override handle.
→ On the ground, you can open the valve with the override handle.

The exception

Rule 3.6 permits the passive in descriptive writing when the agent is genuinely unknown. The standard's own example: During transmission, the data was corrupted. Forcing that active produces Transmission corrupted the data, which names the wrong agent and is now factually wrong. An honest passive beats a false active. (If you must go active, something is a legitimate agent: During transmission, something corrupted the data.)

The same sentence, two ways

When a passive sentence contains an auxiliary verb, the fix depends on what kind of text you are writing. Decide first, then convert.

Non-STEProceduralDescriptive
The volume control can be adjusted. Adjust the volume control. You can adjust the volume control.
The oil temperature must be adjusted before the start of the test. Before you start the test, adjust the oil temperature. Before the start of the test, the operator must adjust the oil temperature.

The companion rule

Rule 3.7

“Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun or other parts of speech.”

Active voice puts the actor in the subject; rule 3.7 puts the action in the verb. They are two halves of the same move.

  • The ohmmeter gives an indication of 450 ohms → The ohmmeter shows 450 ohms.
  • Before the removal of the unit… → Before you remove the unit…

Where the rules collide. STE also says you may only use a word as a verb if the dictionary approves it as one. “Check” is approved as a noun, not a verb — so the standard writes Do a check of the laptop battery, the exact nominalisation rule 3.7 warns about. Rule 3.1 wins inside STE. Outside it, in your own prose, prefer check the battery.
